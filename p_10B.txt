How It Works
The smart street light uses an LDR (Light Dependent Resistor) to detect ambient light levels:

During the day, when there is sufficient sunlight, the LDR senses high light intensity. The circuit keeps the LED (representing the street light) turned off.

At night, when light levels drop, the LDR’s resistance changes. This triggers the transistor to allow current flow, switching the LED on automatically.

This simple yet effective design mimics how real smart street lights operate saving energy by turning on only when needed.

Components Used
• Transistor BC547: Acts as a switch, controlling the LED based on signals from the LDR.

• LDR (Light Dependent Resistor): Detects light intensity and changes resistance accordingly.

• LED: Represents the street light, glowing at night when triggered.

• Resistors: Provide proper current control and protect the components.

What We Learn

• Practical Use of LDRs: We see how light-dependent resistors can be applied in real-world automation, such as street lighting.
